# Production Spec — Reference Ad Teardown

**Reference ad:** Chicnutrix "Glow Advanced" — influencer/UGC performance ad
**Public link:** [Meta Ad Library — ad ID 1393506182719580](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?id=1393506182719580)
**Runtime:** 45.6 s · 720×1280 (9:16 vertical) · 30 fps · 17 shots
**Analysed by:** frame-accurate scene-cut detection (threshold 0.25) + shot-by-shot frame extraction

---

## 1. What kind of video this is

A 45-second vertical direct-response ad for an ingestible beauty supplement, built as a **single-presenter confessional** — one woman talks straight to camera for the whole runtime while B-roll and product footage cut in over her voice. It opens on a 8-second contrarian hook (*"The worst thing Instagram ever did was convince me that every skin problem needed a different product"*) that attacks the category before it sells anything.

The job it does for the brand: **reframe the buyer's problem so the product becomes the obvious answer.** It is not an awareness film. It argues that a shelf full of topical products is the wrong solution, then positions one ingestible as the replacement — and closes on a time-bound proof claim ("in 6 weeks* only") designed to drive a click, not a feeling.

---

## 2. How it's shot and why

| Choice | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Single fixed medium shot, waist-up, eye-level** for every A-roll beat | Costs nothing to shoot, and the unchanging frame makes the presenter feel like a person talking to you rather than an ad. Stability is also what lets 6 separate A-roll segments cut together as one continuous conversation. |
| **Same room, same outfit, same lighting for 100% of A-roll** | The ad cuts away 10 times. Every return to the presenter must feel like *no time has passed* — one wardrobe and one setup is what buys that. |
| **Soft warm key from frame-left, visible palm-frond shadow on a beige wall** | Reads as "someone's apartment in the afternoon," not a studio. The shadow is doing heavy lifting: it's a fixed, recognisable background feature that proves continuity across every cutback. |
| **Extreme close-up B-roll of real skin texture** (pigmentation, breakouts, pores) | The problem has to look real and unretouched, or the promise is not credible. These are the only shots in the ad that are deliberately unflattering. |
| **Fast cutting through the problem list — 4 shots in 4.2 seconds (6.7 s → 10.8 s)** | The cutting rate *is* the argument. Each rapid cut is one more product you supposedly need; the edit makes the category feel exhausting before the script says so. |
| **Cutting slows to 3–4.7 s per shot after the product appears** | Pace drop signals relief. The problem section is frantic; the solution section breathes. |
| **CG liposome renders (28.9 s → 36.8 s, 7.9 s total — the longest single block)** | Supplements can't show results in action, so abstract science imagery substitutes for proof. It also buys the ad its most "premium" visual without a production budget. |
| **Kinetic caption on every single frame, mixed serif-italic and sans** | Built for sound-off autoplay. The italic serif carries the emotional words, the sans carries the claims — so the hierarchy survives even as a thumbnail. |
| **Before/after split at 39.2 s, held only 1.7 s** | Placed late, after credibility is built, and held short — long enough to register, short enough not to invite scrutiny. |
| **3.4-second logo-only end card** | Standard performance-ad practice: brand last, once the argument has landed. |

## 4. What must never change

These are locked. Any future video in this series that breaks one of them stops feeling like part of the same family.

**Character**
- One presenter, female, mid-20s, South Asian, shoulder-length dark hair worn down
- Same face across every A-roll shot — this is the single hardest constraint and the first thing a viewer notices when it breaks
- One outfit per video, saturated single-colour, spaghetti-strap — never patterned
- Delivers to lens, never to an off-camera interviewer

**Environment**
- One room per video, shot from one camera position for all A-roll
- Warm beige / sand wall, visibly textured, never pure white
- Hard plant-shadow cast frame-left — the continuity anchor
- Nothing in frame changes position between cutbacks

**Look**
- Warm palette only: beige, sand, terracotta, skin tones, plus one saturated accent (here, magenta) carried by wardrobe
- Soft directional key from frame-left, no hard shadow on the face, one lighting logic for the whole video
- 9:16, 720×1280 minimum, 30 fps

**Structure**
- Hook 8–10 s, single unbroken A-roll, no cut inside it
- CTA 3–4 s, logo-only, no presenter
- Mid-section beats never exceed 10 s
- Captions on 100% of frames — the ad must work muted

**Tone**
- First person, conversational, mild contrarian irritation — never bubbly, never clinical
- The problem is named before the product appears
- One time-bound claim near the end, asterisked

---

## 5. Three questions for the client

1. **Where does this run — Meta Reels, YouTube pre-roll, or Amazon PDP?** Reels can carry an 8-second hook before the payoff; a skippable YouTube pre-roll needs the product visible before 0:05, which would restructure the entire first third.

2. **What claims has your regulatory/legal team already cleared, and what does the "6 weeks*" asterisk resolve to?** The whole ad is built on a time-bound efficacy claim. If that number isn't substantiated for this market, the script's spine changes, not just its wording.

3. **Is this presenter a contracted brand face for the series, or one of a rotating cast?** A locked face means we build a reusable character reference set and every future video inherits it; a rotating cast means consistency lives in the room and the grade instead, which is a different build.
